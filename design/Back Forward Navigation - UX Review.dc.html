1Findings: one double‑click on #2525 moved the sidebar about 5,000px, and nothing remembers where you were

PwrGit has no history. The selection is a single {repoId, worktreeId} in App.tsx, so a jump replaces it outright. Worse, the jump's own reveal rearranges the sidebar under you. Every specimen here is light theme at shipped size, drawn from app.css. PR numbers and branch names follow the PwrAgnt sidebar in the screenshot that prompted this review, the PR titles are completed by hand, and everything else is invented. Artboards load Google's “Geist”, so text widths can differ from the app's bundled Geist Sans by a few px.

1aFour findings
1

There is no way back.

The only memory of past places is focus‑visits, a worktree → last‑viewed‑time map that ranks the Focused lens. It records when, not in what order, and nothing in the UI can return you to it.

2

The reveal forces open the 190‑row disclosure, and saves that.

When the selection lands in the remaining worktrees, RepoRow runs setWorktreesOpen(true). It also persists the result to pwrgit.unpinnedWorktreesOpen.<repo>. With 199 worktrees, the Pull requests list you clicked from is now about 190 rows lower, and it is still that low after the next launch.

3

Every jump already goes through one hub.

That covers the sidebar's onRevealWorktree, the palette, lineage chips, the title‑bar branch switcher, tag Locate, and clone/fork completion. All of them end in setSelection, some through pendingReveal. So recording history in one place, by watching the selection the way PwrAgnt watches its location, covers all twelve entry points without touching a single call site.

4

The “View all…” dialog can't do everything the short list can.

The dialog's PR New worktree calls onCreateWorktree without the PR, so the new‑worktree sheet loses its subject. The dialog's rows also ignore double‑click, which the sidebar uses to reveal a worktree or switch. Both are split out as a separate task: a dialog that can only see the rest of the list, not act on it, is not much use.

2Back and Forward in the title bar: PwrAgnt's pair, in the same place relative to the crumbs

PwrAgnt places .history-nav at the leading edge of the header, just before what it navigates between. In PwrGit that spot is between the brand and the repo › branch crumbs. These are the same component and class names as PwrAgnt's, and the same 28×24 chip that rests muted and tints on hover. The window is a drag region, so the pair needs no‑drag. Shortcuts go in the tooltip, never inside the chip.

3What Back remembers, and where it puts your eyes

The model is PwrAgnt's useNavigationHistory, ported almost as‑is. It watches the current place and records changes, with a back stack, a cursor, and a forward stack. Each stack is capped at 50, and consecutive repeats are collapsed. A new place clears Forward. A place here is a worktree selection. A few smaller things travel with the entry instead of becoming entries of their own, so that clicking down the lineage graph does not bury the worktree you came from under 30 commit stops.

4Ghost rows: show the one you're on, without opening the 190

When the place you land on (by Back, Forward, or any jump) lives behind a cap, today's fix is to expand the cap: the disclosure opens, or the lens widens through showRepoRow. A ghost does the opposite. The list keeps its shape, and the one row you need shows up at the edge of the visible block, nearest to where it really lives, with a dashed outline that says “visiting”. It is a real row, not a picture of one: same component, same click, same menu, same ★.

Compare 1b: the same jump, and #2525 is still under your pointer, with the worktree it opened four rows above it. The ghost adds one row above the pointer, so the sidebar compensates scrollTop by that row’s height. The rows above slide up by 26px, and nothing beneath the pointer moves.

The tag is “↓ Other”, pointing at the disclosure directly below. Its tooltip says why the row is here and how to keep it. At the 260px sidebar it shrinks to the arrow alone (4c).

The disclosure is left alone. It stays closed if you had it closed, and its stored open state is never written by a jump, which fixes finding 2 on its own. If you open it yourself, the ghost goes away and the real row inside is selected.

In ref lists the ghost sits after the slice and just above “View all”, behind a dashed “visiting” rule. It goes there rather than in its sorted spot because the slice is a ranking, and slotting an outsider into it would claim it ranks. It shows the same row content as its neighbours, and the same verbs: copy, ⇄, the ⌂ holder chip, +. It is not a summary.

AppearsThe selected worktree, or the anchor Back restores, has no rendered row because it is behind a closed disclosure, past a slice, or in a repo the lens hides. At most one per section.
