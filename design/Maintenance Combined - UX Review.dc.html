1 Combined mode as built — a form, then a run that doesn’t say where it is

The combined tab chains three jobs the other tabs already do one at a time: remove finished worktrees, delete finished local branches, then collect garbage. The plan is a column of checkboxes. The run borrows each tab’s view as it goes. Nothing on screen holds the sequence together, so after the worktree review ends and branch scanning starts, the dialog looks like the Local branches tab running on its own, with no sign that a third step is still to come. The worktree step’s own copy replaces the dialog’s lead, the only step marker is an 11 px muted line, and the one repository with something to review sits among 81 cards that say 0 finished branches · 0 kept.

1bFindings
1

The sequence is invisible once it starts

Upcoming steps are not shown anywhere, and completed ones exist only as bullets. The Combined tab stays lit while each step borrows another tab’s view, so the tab strip contradicts the content.

2

The plan reads as a form, not a sequence

Three unnumbered checkboxes. Nothing says where the run will stop for you: worktrees always pause, branches pause only in Review mode, and collection never pauses.

3

Three spellings of one age guard

Keep branches touched within here, Keep branches touched in the last on Local branches, and Protect recently touched worktrees · Within the last on Worktrees, with different notches (1/3/7/14/30/90 against 1/7/14/30). The branch rules also lose the Never offered box from Finished Branches 4a and become a five-line paragraph.

4

Every repository gets a card, alphabetically

The branch step lists all 82 repositories, including the dozens that report “0 finished branches · 0 kept”. With 382 finished branches spread over a handful of repositories, you have to scroll the whole list to find the ones that propose something. Nothing shows which repositories they are, or how many each one proposes, before you reach its card.

5

Two voices

“Propose Worktrees to Prune”, “Start Pruning and Continue” and “Continue without Pruning” are Title Case. The rest of the dialog is sentence case. “Continue without Pruning” is really skip, but it carries the same visual weight as the action.

6

The review forgets the rules it applied

You set the age guard, PR proof and branch mode on the plan, and then the plan is gone. At the moment you are asked to delete 382 branches, nothing on screen says they were filtered by touched in the last 7 days, or that never-pushed branches, branches held by a worktree and protected names were already excluded. The worktree review repeats its protection window only because it is that tab’s own box.

7

The Worktrees tab drifted from its siblings

The sweep result sits in an accent-filled bar that reads like a warning, where the sibling tabs use a neutral status card with a check. Select all has a bordered box to itself. Every candidate is its own card and turns red when checked. The dialog also widens from 720 to 860 px when you switch to it.

2 One anatomy for four tabs

Each tab is built from the same six parts in the same order: rules → never box → status card → rules in force → Finished / Kept → footer. Once a review is on screen the rules fieldset is gone, so a one-line rules in force strip restates it right above the list you are about to act on. Combined adds one more part, the step rail, and otherwise reuses the parts of whichever step is running. The words match across tabs too. Both reviews say Finished and Kept, and both age guards read “Keep … touched in the last [n days]”.

3 Combined, redrawn — the plan becomes the rail

Before the run, the three steps are numbered cards, each carrying its own rules and a tag that says whether it pauses. After Start, the same three steps shrink into a rail that stays under the tabs until the run ends. The rail always answers three questions: which step is running, what the finished steps did, and what is still to come. Everything under the rail belongs to the current step and uses that step’s parts from turn 2. All data is invented.

Restore recreates a branch at the tip it had when it was deleted, for as long as Git still holds that commit. Removed worktrees cannot be restored.

When the run finishes, the rail becomes the receipt: one card per step, in order, each with its result, and the three steps can be expanded independently. 4 Worktrees tab, brought in line with its siblings

Garbage collection and Local branches already match Finished Branches turn 4, and they stay as they are. Worktrees is the tab that drifted, so it gets the same parts. Its protection box becomes a Finished worktrees fieldset worded like the branch one. The sweep result moves into the neutral status card. Candidates become Finished rows in one card, and the protected ones become Kept chips counted by reason.
